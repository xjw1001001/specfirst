# ADR-008: The choices of read-1a (the scorer for the adverse event summary)

Status: decided in read-1a, 2026-11-17 and 18, by the executor, where
PLAN.md and ADR-005 are silent. PLAN.md, `standard/schemas/` and the catalog
`catalog@1` are not changed. Decisions 5, 7, 8 and 9 were changed after
pass 1 of the independent review (docs/reports/read-1a.review.md, findings
R1 to R3). Decisions 7 and 8 are how this part reads the text of its own
gate: that reading is the owner's to accept, and the last section records
the question and the answer. The other decisions stand unless the owner
objects. Three more points are for the owner; they are listed before it.

## Context

read-1a is the scorer of PLAN.md section 9 for the slice of ADR-005: the
reading score and the build score for the decisions of four units and for
one output, attribution, the arms that need no reader, and the planted
defects of section 9.5. Its gate (ADR-005, decision 1): "The planted
defects of section 9.5 that apply to the slice are each detected and
attributed to the right layer. The parser control scores 100%." The work
started on a hand-made study and on the pilot and finished on the kits of
gen-2a (section 13.6).

The plan says what is scored. It does not say how two tables are held
against each other, how a candidate that states a part of the decisions
is built, what owns a wrong cell when several things differ, what a
planted defect of section 9.5 is in a table of counts and percents, or
what "detected" means on a kit that cannot show a decision.

## Decisions

1. **Gold is built, not read.** The scorer builds the gold decisions of a
   study on each dataset and compares the candidate's build with that
   build. Both are made by the same code, so two builds differ only where
   the decisions do (rule R4), and attribution has the artifacts of both
   sides: compiled specs, ADaM, results, layout, grid.

   Reused (rule R2): the compiler of std-1; the yamaa runner, the Python
   result engine and the graph builder of the machine; and from gen-2a the
   listing of the groups of a data-driven grouping, the results on listed
   groups and the grid builder for layout draft 0
   (`gold.resolve_groupings`, `gold.python_results`,
   `specfirst.gen.display`; ADR-007, decisions 1 to 3). The stand-in grid
   builder of the first half (`specfirst.score.display0`) is removed. On a
   kit, `specfirst.score.kit.gold_problems` holds the scorer's build of the
   gold decisions against the files of the kit: the grid as a whole, every
   result, every value of every ADaM column. If they differ, the kit or
   the scorer is wrong and nothing is scored.

   Not chosen: `gold.Workbench` of gen-2a as the whole build. It has no
   place where a planted defect can change what one step hands to the next
   (the ARS file, the ADaM tables, the results), and it gives no
   `derives_from` edges. Not chosen: the files of the kit as the gold
   build. A wrong cell would then be compared with artifacts that another
   run made.

2. **What a row and a column are.** A row of gold and a row of the
   candidate are the same row when they have the same section and the same
   label (the n-th such row when a label repeats); a column is its header
   text. Position does not count for the cells: a candidate that leaves
   out the second row still has the third. Order counts for the strict
   score only. The four scores of ADR-005, decision 4 are computed on
   this, each with its denominator named in the output.

3. **The vector that stands for a candidate.** Inside the scored units a
   decision takes the candidate's value, else the house default, else
   nothing (ADR-005, decision 3). Outside them it takes the gold value: a
   slice does not score what it does not read. A decision that the study
   does not make (gold has no record of it, because the catalog entry does
   not apply to its design) takes no default either.

4. **Stage T is built on the ADaM of gold.** Stage T (ADR-005, decision 2)
   gives both vectors the ADaM datasets that the gold decisions build, and
   scores the table decisions only. A case that scores a module unit at
   stage T is refused.

5. **Attribution: every layer makes a claim, and a claim is run before it
   is an owner** (section 9.4: "the first element that differs owns the
   error"). For a wrong cell the walk goes from the cell to what it rests
   on: the results it shows, their analyses, the ADaM columns those read,
   the columns these derive from.

   First: does anything above the display reach the cell? Only when a
   result of the cell has another value in the candidate or is not there,
   or when a group of a result is another group (a treatment label written
   in another way). When every result and every group of the cell is the
   same in both builds, the difference is the display's, whatever differs
   further up.

   Where something reaches the cell, each layer with a difference on the
   path makes a claim, the far end first: the ADaM columns (L4); the
   analyses with what their definitions name (L5); the results (L6, which
   names no decision); the layout (L7). A build that stopped is claimed by
   the layer `build`, with the rule that failed. A claim names, of the
   decisions in which the candidate differs from gold, those that decide
   its element: the `decides` edge of section 4.2, proven by compiling
   both values.

   Then every claim is run (rule R1): the candidate is built again with
   decisions at their gold values, and a claim holds for a cell when the
   cell then has the gold text. The owner is the first claim of which one
   decision alone gives the gold text, or else all its decisions; else the
   first claim with all the decisions of all claims, when only that gives
   the gold text (more than one difference reaches the cell); else the
   first claim that names no decision, when the gold decisions themselves
   do not give the gold text in this build (a step of the build was
   changed); else the first claim, not confirmed. An owner that is not
   confirmed is a claim that failed: the reports count it apart, and it is
   read as "no owner was found".

   An axis that is not the gold axis is walked and run the same way; the
   columns of a table rest on the ADaM columns that the analyses read as a
   grouping or as an analysis set.

   A cell has one owner. A wrong decision that owns nothing confirmed is
   built alone on gold and listed: `silent` when that changes nothing on
   any dataset, "behind another owner" with the cells it changes alone.

   The first version of this decision took the far-end claim as the owner
   and ran it afterwards. With one wrong decision that is the same thing.
   With two it gave a cell of the display to a module whenever one record
   of an ADaM column on the path differed: in 464 of 2,018 wrong cells of
   120 candidates of the reviewer (finding R2).

6. **A decision that the data cannot show is compared by value and
   reported apart** (section 9.3; ADR-007, "The owner's answer on decision
   6"). The reading score compares the values of every decision. A wrong
   value that owns no cell is built alone on gold: when it changes no cell
   and no axis of any dataset, the report lists it as `silent` (these data
   cannot show it). The scorer finds this by building. On a kit, what it
   finds is held against the identifiability matrix of the kit, pair by
   pair: the scorer and the kit must agree on whether the twin separates a
   value from gold.

7. **The planted defects of section 9.5 in this slice.** Twelve are
   listed. How each is planted, and at which layer it must be owned:

   | Defect of section 9.5 | Here | Layer |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | arms exchanged in one analysis | the results of two arms of one analysis are exchanged after the engine | L6 |
   | a wrong analysis set | (a) the table names another set (`tfl.population`); (b) the set is formed by another rule (a decision of mod-populations) | L5; L4 |
   | a window off by one day | treatment emergence ends one day later (`ae.teae.end`) | L4 |
   | a wrong denominator | in the ARS file, the percent of the second row divides by the subjects counted in the first row | L5 |
   | a missing column | in the layout, the total column is left out | L7 |
   | a changed quantile definition | does not apply: the table has no quantile and nothing near one | - |
   | a p-value rounded twice | the nearest thing: every percent is rounded to a whole number after the engine, and the grid rounds that again to one decimal | L6 |
   | a baseline taken from the wrong record | does not apply: the table has no baseline value and nothing near one | - |
   | an unstable sort | the nearest thing, the order of the treatment columns: (a) read as a decision (`tfl.columns.arms` takes its other value); (b) wrong in the build (the layout orders by the other rule, every decision is gold) | L7; L7, no decision |
   | a frozen site list (caught on the twin only) | the nearest thing: the list of related causality values, cut down to those the visible data hold (`ae.related.values`) | L4 |
   | a quote that is not in the document | planted in a citation | L0 |
   | a citation that points at the wrong span | planted in a citation | L0 |

   Ten of the twelve are planted, two of them in two ways: twelve plants.
   Which of them "apply to the slice" is decided by one criterion: a
   defect applies when the table has the thing, or has something near it
   that can go wrong in the same way. Three are planted as the nearest
   thing. The table has no p-value, but it has a number that is rounded
   for display, and that can be rounded twice. It sorts one thing, its
   treatment columns; a sort that changes from run to run is for property
   P1 to find. It has no site list; its list of data values is the list of
   related causality values. Two have nothing near them: no quantile, no
   baseline value.

   Four plants are in a step of the build and not in a decision (arms
   exchanged, wrong denominator, missing column, the order of the columns
   in the layout), and one in the results (the percent rounded twice): no
   decision of the slice does these, and the attribution must then name
   the layer and no decision.

   Where the visible data of a study hold every value of the list of
   related causality values (every generated kit of the gate set), the
   frozen list is planted with visible data that lack the adverse event
   records of the first value: a reader that froze the list saw less. The
   twin is never changed. On such a kit "caught on the twin only" is true
   of the visible data by construction; what the plant tests there is that
   the twin shows it and that the owner is the decision. On HAND01 the
   visible data lack a value as they are.

   A planted defect counts as detected when the score it must lower is
   lower, and as attributed when every wrong cell and every axis of the
   candidate has an owner, at the layer the defect was planted at and,
   where a decision was planted, that decision, confirmed.

8. **The gate on the kits.** The check `kits of gen-2a` writes the ten
   kits of the development seed of the gate gen-2a again (a kit is never
   in the repository, and it is a pure function of the generator, the
   catalog, the house standard and the seed), and passes when

   - the files of every kit have the hashes of its manifest;
   - on every kit the gold built again is the gold of the kit;
   - on every kit the parser control scores 100%, at stage C and at stage T;
   - no planted defect fails on any kit: one that shows is attributed to
     its layer; one that no cell shows is `silent`, and the matrix of the
     kit says the same of the twin;
   - every defect that applies to the slice is detected and attributed on
     at least one kit;
   - the sweep has no problem (below);
   - the checks above fail on what they are for: a gold grid with another
     text, a document without one statement, a matrix that says the
     opposite of one pair.

   On a kit a defect can also be `not planted`: a window off by one day
   needs a gold decision that names a window of days, a missing total
   column needs a total column, a frozen list needs a list of two values,
   the other order of the columns needs two orders that differ. On HAND01
   nothing may be `silent` or `not planted`: the study was made so that
   every plant shows, and check 9 of the gate fails otherwise.

9. **The sweep of the matrix** (beyond the text of the gate). The matrix
   of a kit lists, for every decision of the slice, every other value a
   study of its design can decide, with the claim whether the twin shows
   it and in how many cells. Each of them is a planted defect that the kit
   itself vouches for. The scorer scores each as a candidate with that one
   wrong decision and must find: the reading score has exactly that
   decision wrong; the build score of the twin falls exactly when the
   matrix says `separated`, and where the axes of the twin are the gold
   axes, in as many cells as the matrix counts; every wrong cell and every
   axis has an owner, the changed decision, at the layer where that
   decision acts (a table in `specfirst.score.kit`, held against the
   compile manifest of a kit by a unit test); a value that shows nowhere
   is reported as `silent`.

   With one wrong decision an owner has no other decision to name, and
   "confirmed" cannot fail. So the sweep goes on to pairs: up to four
   module decisions that the twin separates, each with up to three table
   decisions, both wrong at once. What each does alone is known from the
   first pass, cell by cell, and the owner of every wrong cell of a pair
   is held against it: the one decision that changes the cell alone; both,
   where each changes it; one of the two where only both together do;
   never a claim that failed.

   Section 9.5 has one defect per kind of error; the sweep has every
   decision, and pairs of them.

10. **The pilot is a report** (ADR-005, decision 5). Arm S at stage T on
    the reference ADaM of CDISCPILOT01, held against the results that
    CDISC's ARS example embeds. A row of the example and a row of the
    house table are the same row when they count the same events: five of
    the eight analyses of the example are mapped (`ROWS` in
    `specfirst.score.pilot`), by a reading of two public definitions.
    Titles, footnotes and the header of the row labels are not compared.
    Nothing is tuned on it and its scores decide nothing in the gate.

## Consequences

- Arm S builds nothing on the chain, in any study. `ae.related.values`
  has no house default in `catalog@1`, ADAE cannot be compiled without
  it, and ADR-005, decision 3 says that what cannot be built without a
  decision counts as not produced. "R against S" on the chain is a
  comparison with zero; arm S says something at stage T only.
- The scorer imports the generator (`specfirst.gen.gold`,
  `specfirst.gen.display`, `specfirst.gen.verify`). When the house
  templates write a layout format that the machine's builder reads
  (ADR-007, "For the owner", point 2), both move together.
- A score on a kit is a score against the generator that wrote the kit. A
  kit of another generator version fails the gold check and is not scored.
- Not done in this part: the three layers of ADaM scoring of section 9.3
  (they need all four table families), the cost dimension (read-2a),
  citations into natural-form documents (the anchors of gen-3a).

## For the owner

1. Arm S on the chain (first consequence). A default for
   `ae.related.values`, or an ADAE that builds without it, is a new
   catalog version or a change of the house standard: the std track. The
   scorer needs no change for either.
2. Decision 9 adds a check to the gate that its text does not ask for. It
   can only make the gate harder to pass.
3. The crosswalk of the pilot (decision 10): which analysis of the
   example is which row of the house table.

Noted for the std track, not a decision of this part (review, finding
R4): the catalog types the slot of `ae.related.values` as a list, and the
statement reads "its causality is one of" the values. The reading score
compares a list in its order, so the values of gold in another order count
as a decision to change. Whether a list of a catalog entry is a set is for
the catalog to say.

## The owner's answer on the reading of the gate

Pass 1 of the independent review did not sign read-1a because of decisions
7 and 8 (finding R1 of `docs/reports/read-1a.review.md`). The part reads
the text of its own gate by a table that it wrote, and lesson 47 gives such
a reading to the owner. The reviewer found the reading reasonable and found
no failure of the scorer behind it; the choice is not the reviewer's to
accept, and not the executor's. The executor added the two plants the
reviewer named (a percent rounded twice; the order of the columns wrong in
the build), stopped before the gate ran on the new code, and put the
question to the owner on 2026-11-18, in the question dialog of the session
of read-1a.

The question and the options were shown in Chinese. Rule R10 keeps this
file in ASCII, so they stand here in the executor's translation; the
owner's own words are in the transcript of the session.

Question: "The gate text of read-1a: 'The planted defects of section 9.5
that apply to the slice are each detected and attributed to the right
layer. The parser control scores 100%.' The independent review did not
sign. Its one blocking finding (R1): 'apply to the slice' and 'each
detected' are read by a table that this part wrote itself, and by lesson 47
that needs your approval. The reviewer finds the reading reasonable and
found no failure of the scorer hidden behind it. The reading, made
stricter as the reviewer suggested: (1) Of the 12 defects, 10 are planted.
Three of them as the nearest thing the table has: no p-value, so a percent
rounded twice; no sort that can be unstable, so the order of the treatment
columns, once as a decision read wrongly and once as a step of the build
gone wrong; no site list, so the list of related causality values. Two do
not apply: a quantile and a baseline (the table has neither, and nothing
near them). (2) On the hand-made study all 12 plants must be detected and
attributed. (3) On the 10 kits no plant may fail. A plant that the data of
a kit cannot show (silent, and the kit's own identifiability matrix must
say the same) or that has nothing to be planted in (not planted) is no
failure. Every defect is detected and attributed on at least one kit; in
fact most on all 10, the window off by one day on 3, the frozen list on 5,
the missing column on 8. Do you approve this reading as the gate of
read-1a?"

Options: "Approve (recommended)", with the description: "The reading as
above. The question, the options and your answer are recorded in ADR-008.
Then the gate runs again on a clean clone, the review signs in its second
pass, and the part is merged into main." And "Not yet", with the
description: "No merge. Write in the note what must change (for example
which defects must apply, or what is asked of a kit); I stop and wait for
it."

Answer: none yet when this was committed. The gate does not run on this
code, and nothing is merged, before it is here.
