## Decisions

1. **A registry kind `model`, R only.** Three entries:
   `model.ancova.lsmeans` (lsmean, se, lower_cl, upper_cl per group),
   `model.ancova.pairwise` (estimate, se, lower_cl, upper_cl, p_value per
   group against a reference group) and `model.ancova.dose_response`
   (p_value, estimate per analysis). Their numbered semantics fix the model
   records, the model, its variance, the LS means and their weights, the
   contrasts, the degrees of freedom and what is missing. A model-based
   entry lists `implementations: [r]`, and the registry refuses anything
   else for it (section 7.3, decision 13 as refined by decision 21).

   Options that change numbers: `factors` and `covariates` (the model
   terms), `weights` (equal, proportional: SAS's OM), `df` (residual,
   asymptotic), `confidence_level` (90, 95, 99), `reference` (the group the
   others are compared with), `dose` (the value of the grouping variable,
   or the rank of the group) and `model` (the dose as a continuous variable,
   or a linear contrast of the treatment factor).

2. **Two option kinds that are not lists of values.** An option may be
   `{takes: columns, default: []}` (names of columns of the analysis
   dataset) or `{takes: group, default: null}` (the ID of a group of the
   analysis's grouping). Only a model-based method has them. An analysis
   states them in `programmingCode` with the context `specfirst method
   registry`, as other options (std-1's convention); a parameter that takes
   columns may have any number of values. The two parameters that name
   columns, `factors` and `covariates`, are known to the ARS reader
   (`ars.MODEL_TERMS`): `ars.reads` gives their columns the role `model`,
   so they are handed to the R engine and have `reads` edges, and every
   cell behind them has a complete trace. The registry refuses an option
   that takes columns under another name.

3. **How a model-based analysis is run (rules I16 to I19 of
   standard/methods/README.md).** The model is fitted once to the analysis
   records; the analysis has one grouping, the treatment; a record's group
   is the group whose where clause it fulfils; the results are one per
   group (results by group) or one per analysis (the grouping compared).
   The R implementation fits with `stats::lm` and takes the LS means, their
   differences and limits from emmeans, the Type III F test of the dose from
   car::Anova.

4. **The gold ARD holds the R engine's rows of model-based methods.** The
   Python engine gives no row for an analysis whose method only R
   implements (`ard.Engine.r_only`); the build adds the R engine's rows of
   those methods to the gold ARD (`build.gold_rows`). Rule R11 is checked on
   the methods both engines implement; for the others the check asks that
   the gold ARD holds the R engine's rows as the R engine gave them.

5. **The vectors' expected values come from an exact computation.**
   `tests/methods/oracle_models.py` solves the normal equations in
   fractions, builds the LS means and contrasts from their coefficient
   vectors as the statements write them, and takes the t distribution from
   its closed form for whole degrees of freedom (Abramowitz and Stegun
   26.7.3 and 26.7.4) in 60-digit decimals, a quantile by bisection. It
   shares nothing with lm, emmeans or car. ADR-012 names "a second R route,
   or published values"; an exact computation outside R is a stronger
   independence than a second R route, and the CSR's tables check the
   methods again. The test `model-vectors` also asks that every option
   value occurs in the vectors, and runs the vectors again with one option
   of each entry fixed at one value in the inputs (what an implementation
   that ignored the option would compute); the vectors must see the
   difference. The defect is planted in the inputs, not in the R code
   (review of real-5a, F6); the reviewer planted defects in the R code
   itself, and the vectors saw them.

6. **Our own reporting event.** A result set of a kit may name an ARS file
   of our own in the kit folder (`gold/events/efficacy-adas.json`), next to
   the pinned ones. It is written from the SAP, the analysis results
   metadata and the CSR's shells, embeds no result and is MIT. Its titles
   and row labels are those of the CSR (short identifiers); its footnotes
   say the method in our own words; no CSR footnote is copied.

7. **The CSR is the reference of a grid.** An output in kit.yaml may name
   a `reference: {csr, page, table}`; the CSR is a pinned file of the kit
   (`gold/reference/csr/CSR.pdf`, Pilot 6, the SHA-256 that base-0
   recorded). `specfirst.machine.csr` turns the page into text with
   poppler's pdftotext (-layout), parses it into a reference grid (titles,
   column labels with their counts, lines of label and cells, footnotes),
   writes it to `build/csr/<kit>/<table>.json` (ignored, outside the fetch
   cache, never in git) and compares the grid of the build with it on
   every check: column labels, titles, the header's counts, every line in
   order (a section of the grid against a line without cells), every cell.
   Labels and cells are compared without their blanks: pdftotext places
   blanks, a pattern pads with them, and SAP template 5 writes "xx.x(x.xx)"
   where the CSR prints "-0.5 (0.82)". Footnotes are not compared.

8. **A proof kind for a difference of display: `other_pattern`.** A cell
   that differs from the CSR needs exactly one entry in the `cells` section
   of the kit's exceptions file, with its class, the decision it is about
   and a proof that `csr.judge` checks. `other_pattern` proves a
   `rule_choice`: the cell's own results, shown by other patterns, give the
   CSR's text, and ours is another. This is the first real case ADR-003
   (decision 5) waited for.

9. **Decisions follow PLAN.md decision 17.** The documents decide; where
   they are silent, the reference ADaM decides; then the house default.
   Hence the confidence limits are shown with 2 decimals, as SAP template 5
   shows them, and the CSR's 1 decimal is a `rule_choice` difference in the
   9 cells of the three tables. The dose-response test has the baseline as
   a covariate (SAP 3.3 and 10.1.1); the Define-XML's code, which leaves it
   out, is listed for the owner, not followed. The row labels are the
   CSR's: SAP template 5 writes "N", "P-value" and "Diff. of LS Means" where
   the CSR writes "n", "p-value" and "Diff of LS Means"; labels are not
   cells, and the comparison holds them against the CSR.

10. **Decision records with custom IDs.** The pilot kit names a file of
    decision records (`build.decisions`, `gold/decisions/efficacy.jsonl`):
    eight records `custom:eff-*`, each fitting decision-record.schema.json.
    The brief asked for "status custom"; the schema has no such status
    (`stated`, `default`, `asked`, `conflict`, `not_applicable`) and gives a
    decision outside the catalog the ID `custom:<slug>` (PLAN.md section
    4.3), so the records are custom by their ID. Their statuses say where
    the information lives: five are `stated` (`document_rule`); two are
    `conflict`, where the documents disagree and section 4.5 asks a person
    (the model of the dose-response test; whether the pairwise comparisons
    are shown when the dose response is not significant), each citing both
    sides and keeping the value the build uses; one is `asked`
    (`study_input`: the Baseline rows, which no document decides). The
    review of real-5a (finding F2) asked for these statuses; the first
    version had all eight `stated`. No compiler joins them to the reporting event yet, so
    `specfirst.machine.decisions` holds them together: every bound element
    exists, and every option a decision sets (model terms, dose, pairwise
    inference, LS-means weights) is the decision's value in every analysis
    it binds. The gate check `decisions` runs it.

11. **Layout spec v0, block kind `statistics`.** Recorded as an amendment
    to ADR-003.

## Consequences

- Files of a generated kit and a reader's task folder: the method registry
  (`standard/methods/`, `registry.json`) has three more entries and two new
  option forms. Every generated kit pins the SHA-256 of `registry.json` in
  its kit.yaml (`specfirst.gen.kits`), so every generated kit's kit.yaml
  changes; every reader task folder copies `registry.json`, so every task
  folder and its ID change. No generated kit uses a model-based method, so
  their data, gold and documents do not depend on the new entries; that is
  not checked byte for byte. No schema, no catalog entry, no house file and
  no skill changes. The kept dry run of arm R of read-2a is tied to the
  registry's bytes and fails the check "arm R, kept dry run" of the gate
  read-2a (docs/reports/real-5a.md; corrected after the review of real-5a,
  finding F1).
- A kit with a model-based analysis needs R to build, as every kit with a
  reporting event does.
- The gates real-2 and real-3a count the results and grids of the ARS
  example's result set (`common-safety-displays`): the kit now has a second
  result set. Their checks are otherwise unchanged.
- real-5b adds the mixed model (`mmrm`) and CMH with the same machinery: a
  registry kind `model` for the mixed model, a pair of implementations for
  CMH (rule R11), more outputs in kit.yaml with a CSR reference.

## Amendment of real-5b (2026-11-19): the mixed model, CMH, and Tables 14-3.11 and 14-3.13

Part real-5b of the slice `efficacy-first` (ADR-012). Decided by the
executor of real-5b, 2026-11-19; it stands unless the owner objects. The
base session relayed the owner's go-ahead ("start real-5b now"); its merge
waited for the read track's ADR that binds a kept reader run to versions:
ADR-017, approved by the owner and carried out on `main` (`7806105`). PLAN.md, `standard/schemas/`, `env/`, `catalog@1` and
the house standard are not changed. The decisions above hold; these are
added.

12. **The mixed model for repeated measures, R only.** Two entries of kind
    `model`: `model.mmrm.lsmeans` and `model.mmrm.pairwise` (11 and 7
    statements). Options: the terms (`factors`, `covariates`, `visit`, one
    column, and `by_visit`, the terms whose effect differs by visit,
    columns that rule I19 reads like the analysis variable),
    `treatment_by_visit` (yes, no), `covariance` (unstructured, toeplitz,
    both linear in their parameters), `df` (kenward_roger_linear,
    satterthwaite), `at` (last: the LS means at the last visit; average:
    averaged over the visits with equal weights), `confidence_level`, and
    `reference` for the comparisons. The fit is mmrm 0.3.19 by restricted
    maximum likelihood with the optimizer nlminb: mmrm's default, L-BFGS-B,
    stops before the maximum and moves standard errors in the fifth digit
    (report of real-5b). The LS means and their differences come from
    emmeans on that fit.
13. **Kenward-Roger as mmrm computes it, stated.** The statements say what
    the implementation computes and the vectors hold: the adjusted
    covariance of Kenward and Roger (1997) for parameters the covariance is
    linear in (mmrm's `Kenward-Roger-Linear`, which does not depend on how
    the covariance is parametrized), and the degrees of freedom 2 (l' V
    l)^2 / (g' W g) with W the inverse of the observed information of the
    restricted likelihood. For one contrast this is Kenward and Roger's
    denominator degrees of freedom with the observed in place of the
    expected information, and Satterthwaite's; the two options differ in
    the standard error.
14. **The vectors of the mixed model: a second optimization.** No exact
    computation exists. tests/methods/oracle_mmrm.py maximizes the
    restricted likelihood by Fisher scoring in numpy, in the entries of the
    covariance matrix, and writes the adjusted covariance and the degrees
    of freedom out from their formulas; it shares no code with mmrm, TMB or
    emmeans. On the CSR's data and on the 23 vectors it agrees with the R
    implementation to about 1e-8 (relative); the vectors are held to 1e-6.
    On a flat likelihood (monotone dropout) it does not converge and agrees
    to about 1e-5 only (review of real-5b, F4): the vectors keep away from
    such data.
    ADR-012 (decision 5) allows "a second R route"; this is a route outside
    R, and the CSR checks the method again on real data.
15. **CMH, Python and R (PLAN.md decision 21).** `test.cmh`: the
    generalized Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel statistic over strata, with the
    option `statistic` (general_association, row_mean_scores), `scores`
    (table: the values; rank: midranks within a stratum) and `strata`
    (columns; a test may now take a columns option). Both implementations
    are written from the statements, the Python one grown from
    `evidence/csr_hylaw_spike.py`; the vectors are computed in fractions
    (the statistic) and the finite series of the chi-square tail. Rule I12d
    says how the tables of a stratified test are made.
16. **A second proof kind, `other_build`** (class `rule_choice`; PLAN.md
    section 4.8: "a build with that alternative, in which the cells equal
    the reference"). The cell built again with the results of other
    analyses of the same event, other patterns, another cell template or
    another display rule gives the CSR's text, and ours is another. It is
    held to "that alternative" (review of real-5b, F1): an other analysis
    may differ from the cell's only in its naming and in the options the
    entry's decisions set (`decisions.PARAMETERS`); another template is a
    cell template of the layout (each part `{name}`, without a format spec
    or a conversion, which could carry text too: review of real-5b, H1),
    shows parts of the cell and adds no text its template has not; an other
    pattern, in either proof kind, changes only the mask of the part's
    pattern and keeps the text before and after it, so that no pattern
    carries the CSR's text (review of real-5b, G1); every decision
    an entry names binds an analysis of the cell or its method (for both
    proof kinds, which also closes finding F3 of the review of real-5a); and
    an entry may name a list of decisions, when the CSR follows the other
    alternative of more than one. The analyses of the other alternative
    stand in the event (reason DATA DRIVEN, purpose exploratory), in no row
    of a layout.
17. **A display rule of its own, `display.pattern.signed`.** The CSR prints
    -0.0399 as "-0.0"; display.pattern drops the sign of a number that
    rounds to zero (its statement 5). The other rule is a registry entry of
    its own (2 statements, Python), not an option of display.pattern: the
    read track's ADR-017 keeps a kept reader run valid while the registry
    entries its slice reaches (count.subjects, percent.subjects,
    display.pattern, the rounding rule) do not change. `custom:eff-mmrm-display`
    states the sign rule gold follows.
18. **Gold of Table 14-3.11: the LS means at Week 24.** SAP 10.1.1 and the
    title of template 10 say Week 24; every number of the CSR follows from
    the LS means averaged over weeks 8, 16 and 24 and none from Week 24
    (Atorus's hint, evidence E22, tested). By decision 9 (PLAN.md decision
    17) gold follows the documents; the 12 cells are classed rule_choice
    with an other_build proof that reads the averaged analyses. The
    records, the degrees of freedom and the CMH statistic, which no
    document settles, keep the values under which the CSR's numbers are
    reproduced, as `conflict` or `asked` records.
19. **Edge cases of the mixed model (review of real-5b, F3).** With one
    group of model records it has its LS mean (the model without the group
    term), with the reference alone the comparisons are missing, and with
    fewer than two visits every result is missing (statement 11 of
    model.mmrm.lsmeans); a vector each.
20. **Display of the two tables: SAP template 10 and ad hoc template 1.**
    The limits of the confidence intervals of 14-3.11 with 2 decimals (the
    CSR: 1); in 14-3.13 a count of 0 as "0 (0%)" and the p-values with 3
    decimals (the CSR: "0" and 4 decimals). Row labels are the CSR's.
