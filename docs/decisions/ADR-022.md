# ADR-022: A calculation-first review view and a study decision sheet (issue #31)

Status: the design was sent to the owner through the base session on
2026-11-21 and accepted with the four recommendations (the section "The
owner's answers"). Carried out on the track real (docs/reports/issue-31.md).
PLAN.md, `standard/schemas/`, `catalog@1`, `catalog@2`, the houses, the
method registry and the kit's reporting events are not changed.

## Context

Issue #31: the trace graph (P3) answers "where does this cell come from",
but a reviewer needs a page that answers the review questions in their
order, with the layout out of the way. The issue asks for an extension of
the viewer of PLAN.md section 7.5:

- the grid, texts and RTF folded by default, the calculation first;
- a click on any number opens a side panel with the analysis in sentences;
  the decisions behind it (standard-form sentence, alternatives not chosen,
  citation, house default or customized); the ADaM variables it reads, each
  with why this variable and its derivation as one sentence, down to SDTM;
  the recomputation of issue #29 with a link to the code that ran;
- a study decision sheet: every study-level decision and template setting,
  each marked `house default` or `study customized`, with its citation;
- three kinds of edge drawn distinctly: proven by execution; judged (a
  citation, which needs a named approver); not proven by the data (P4
  unseparated).

Its acceptance, on CDISCPILOT01's demographics and AE tables: every printed
number opens the panel; every panel reaches SDTM and a citation or a house
default; the decision sheet lists every study-level decision.

What the pilot had: the viewer of section 7.5 was not built (render.py
writes one grid as a page). Of CDISC's example only the adverse event
summary (Out14-3-1-1) was built as a grid; the demographics (Out14-1-1) and
the table by organ class and term (Out14-3-2-1) had results and no grid,
for want of two layout kinds (the rest of real-3). The pilot is built from
Pilot 7's yamaa specifications and CDISC's ARS file, not compiled from
decisions: its only decision records were the 18 custom records of the
efficacy tables, and kit.yaml named no catalog and no house.

Rule R2: no component to reuse was found. tfrmt (ADR-003) formats a table
and has no trace; siera (ADR-002, ADR-021) writes code, not a review page;
the viewer is ours by PLAN.md section 7.1 ("one static HTML page per kit:
build, small").

## Decisions

1. One static page per kit, build/kits/<kit>/viewer/index.html, written by
   `specfirst view <kit>` from a build (`specfirst.viewer`). It has no
   script and loads nothing: a fold is `<details>`, and a panel is a
   section shown by CSS when it is the target of the address or holds it
   (`:target`, `:has(:target)`). One panel per analysis holds what its
   cells share; every cell has its own block in it, shown when the number
   is clicked. The same build gives the same bytes. `specfirst.viewer.check`
   holds the page: no script, handler, frame, image, form or import; every
   link to an anchor of the page; every anchor once.

2. The panel of a number, in the issue's order:
   1. the analysis in sentences, as `specfirst.recompute.reading` reads it
      for the scripts of issue #29: the analysis set and its condition, the
      records and the data subset, the groupings (defined groups with their
      conditions, or one group per value that occurs), the method and its
      operations with the denominator of a percent, and every option with
      where its value comes from (the registry default, the kit's binding,
      the ARS `programmingCode`); and the page references the ARS file
      gives for it (decision 5);
   2. the decision records that bind the analysis or one of its elements
      (`binds`), then those that bind a column it reads or a column one of
      them derives from, down to SDTM, each saying which column it binds
      and through which column read (the review of issue #31, F2: the AE
      start-date imputation stands in the panel of every TEAE count); each
      with its statement, value, status, class, citations, the statements
      of the alternatives of its catalog entry it does not take, its mark
      and its P4 class;
   3. the ADaM columns it reads, with their roles (coverage.json): why this
      column (the records that bind it, or "no decision record binds it:
      the ARS analysis names it and the study spec builds it"); its
      derivation in the study spec's own words, the Define-XML comment of
      the resolved specification, labelled as such (the owner's answer to
      Q3), next to the rule that runs with its file and line; the columns
      it derives from, each the same way; the SDTM columns it reaches; its
      candidate citations (Pilot 7's claims);
   4. the recomputation: its line of the report of issue #29 and the text
      of the script, in a fold.

3. The study decision sheet: every decision record of the kit; every entry
   of the catalog the kit names (`standard`) for every unit, where no
   record takes it, with its question and whether the house has a default;
   the kit's bindings of registry options; and the layout of every output.
   A record is `house default` when its value is the default of the house
   the kit names for its unit, otherwise `study customized`; a custom
   record is always `study customized`. A binding is `house default` when
   an alternative of the catalog compiles to the same option and value
   (its compile template's parameters) and that alternative is the house's
   default (the quantile definition sas5); a layout is the kit's own file
   and `study customized`.

4. The kinds of an edge, drawn apart and never merged: executed (`shows`,
   `computes`); static (`reads`, `derives_from`); judged (`cites`, and a
   record's citations), with "awaiting approval" until a person approves it
   (the owner's answer to Q4: nobody yet); not proven by the data (a
   decision whose P4 class is `unseparated`); tested (another class of a P4
   run: separated, inert, separated at dataset level only); not tested (no
   P4 run reaches the decision). The page check holds that every citation
   is drawn judged and says in its own words that it awaits approval or who
   approved it, and every decision is drawn with the P4 class the review
   gives it.

5. The graph gains, within graph schema version 0: the page references of
   an ARS file (`documentRefs`, `pageRefs` with `pageNumbers` and a
   `label`) of an analysis and of its method, as `cites` edges from the
   analysis to a span `doc:<document>#<page>/<label>` of a kit document,
   judged, imported, `approved_by` null, and `quote_verified` true only
   where a script finds the label on that page (poppler's pdftotext). A
   kit names a reference document of an ARS file in a document's
   `cited_as`: CDISC's example names the SAP CDISCPILOT01_SAP, and its
   sap.pdf has the SHA-256 of the kit's SAP. A reference without page
   numbers (the example's nine adverse event analyses cite "11.2. Adverse
   Events" so) names the document as a whole: its span is
   `doc:<document>#<label>`, its quote is verified where the label stands
   anywhere in the document's text, and coverage.json counts these edges
   (`edges_to_a_whole_document`). A reference to a document the kit does
   not hold (the CSR, a table shell) gives no edge and is counted in
   coverage.json (`documents`).

6. Two layout pieces (ADR-003, amendment of issue #31), for the
   demographics and the table by organ class and term of CDISC's example
   (the owner's answer to Q2), with the rows and columns of its table
   shells (Demog_Table_Shell.rtf, AE_SOC_PT_Table_Shell.rtf in the pinned
   ARS repository):
   - the extra cells of a groups block, in its first row: the p-value of a
     test of the table of the column grouping and the row grouping (the
     chi-square of age group, sex, ethnicity and race);
   - the block kind `terms`: one row per value of a data-driven row
     grouping that the results have, sorted A to Z (`sort: alphabetical`),
     each followed by the rows of a nested data-driven grouping that occur
     with it (`nested`), with extra cells in every row from comparisons
     split by the same groupings (the Fisher p-values of placebo against
     each active arm); a comparison without a result for a row leaves its
     cell empty; a mock shell has one placeholder row per level;
   - an extra column may have a `spanner`.

   Their cells are compared with the example's formatted values (P2 for
   grid text). On Out14-1-1, 14 cells differ by a result the section
   `results` of gold/exceptions.yaml already classes `reference_error`.
   One cell of Out14-3-2-1 differs by display only: a Fisher
   p-value of 1 that the example shows as "1" under its pattern X.XXXX,
   where its other p-values have four decimals. A new section of
   gold/exceptions.yaml, `formats`,
   holds such a difference: the reference's raw value is ours, and its
   formatted value is not that raw value under the pattern of its operation
   (proof `not_its_own_pattern`, held by `grid.compare_reference`); an entry
   that no cell differs by fails, and so does an entry under a key that is
   no result set of the kit with a grid. The example embeds one Fisher
   p-value per test analysis, so 408 results of the table by organ class
   and term have no formatted value: their cells are counted and not
   compared (`gates.PILOT_GRIDS` names the number).

7. The pilot's decision records of the demographics and AE tables (the
   owner's answers to Q1 and to the overlap with std-2): the file
   kits/cdiscpilot01/gold/decisions/safety.jsonl, 49 records over
   `catalog@2` and `open-default@2`, the second decisions file of the kit.
   - 44 records of catalog entries: every entry and unit of the seven units
     the three tables use (mod-treatment, mod-populations, mod-demographics,
     mod-ae-emergence, tfl-demographics, tfl-ae-summary, tfl-ae-soc-pt),
     but four. pop.efficacy.rule and demo.bmi.rule have no row of these
     tables (the efficacy set is custom:eff-analysis-set); demo.baseline.record
     and tfl.decimals.rule have no alternative that fits the build, so
     their decisions are custom: the baseline height and weight rows of the
     build (custom:demog-baseline-visits) and the decimals of CDISC's
     example (custom:demog-decimals). The sheet lists the four as entries
     without a record.
   - 5 custom records (section 10): those two, the two age groups of the
     table (custom:demog-age-display), the Fisher tests of the table by
     organ class and term (custom:socpt-fisher), and the p-value format of
     that table (custom:socpt-pvalue-format: catalog@2's tfl.pvalue.format
     is of the demographics only).
   - The documents read: the SAP's text (sections 6, 7, 9, 11, the list of
     its tables in 13.1.1) and its mock tables (13.2: Template 3 for the
     demographics, Template 13 for the table by organ class and term; the
     SAP has none for the adverse event summary), and the Define-XML of
     ADaM. A mock table states what it shows: its population, its columns
     and their order, and the decimals of its masks ("0.xxx", "xx (xx%)",
     "x.xx"), the owner's answer of 2026-11-22. The SAP itself says of its
     templates (13.1.3): "Note that the templates that follow are for
     example only." The first review of issue #31 (F1) found that the
     records had not read them.
   - The status says where the value comes from: `stated` when a kit
     document states it, with its quote (18); `default` when the value is
     the house's default and no kit document states it (12: it cites the
     house standard's statement by its anchor, doc `house`, the STANDARD.md
     of open-default@2, as `specfirst.compile.house.default_decisions`
     writes a default; the page also shows the sources the house gives for
     that default in its defaults.yaml, and says so where it gives none);
     `conflict` when a kit document says other than the build does (17: the
     record keeps the build's value and cites the document; a person
     decides; a rule of the build that names no visit where the documents
     name one, as for the baseline height and weight, is a conflict too,
     whether or not the two give the same values on the pilot); `asked`
     when no document states it and the value is not the house's default
     (2, class `gold_quirk`: CDISC's example does it, no source of the kit
     supports it). The conflicts and the asked records are for the owner
     (docs/reports/issue-31.md lists each).
   - The decisions check holds them (`specfirst.machine.decisions`): a
     record of a catalog entry fits the catalog the kit names (its entry,
     its unit, its value an alternative with its slots, its statement the
     one the catalog renders, a `default` the house's default, of one case
     where the default depends on the design); every element it binds is an
     ARS element or output of a reporting event, or a column of the built
     ADaM; a stated record cites a quote; every quote of a document of the
     kit or of the house standard stands in it (poppler's pdftotext for a
     PDF, the text of an XML or Markdown file). A citation of a source the
     check names (`decisions.OTHER_SOURCES`) is counted and not checked: five
     efficacy records cite the owner's answers of 2026-11-20 (doc `answers`,
     ADR-015), which are no document of the kit; the page says that no
     script checks them. A citation of any other name fails. The 18 efficacy
     records are held by the same rules.
   - What it does not hold: a record is not held by result. Whether the
     reference build does what a value says, the compiled fragment of the
     value against the built ADaM, is std-2's check (PLAN.md section 13.4).
     std-2 takes this file over, extends it to the whole pilot and adds that
     check; nothing here claims it.
   - The records are gold: no reader's task folder holds them (the pilot's
     task writes an empty gold/decisions.jsonl and a manifest of its own,
     specfirst.harness.pilot), and no score reads them.
   - The kit format (the owner's answer of 2026-11-21, ADR-001 item 3):
     `build.decisions` is a file or a list of files; kit.yaml has `standard:
     {catalog, house}`, the labels of the frozen versions the records are
     written over.

8. P4 on the page: a decision is drawn with its class from a P4 matrix
   (`specfirst view --p4 FILE`, the `decisions` of `specfirst gen
   separate`), and "not tested" without one. The matrix of the pilot is
   read after catalog-2c merges, so that its classes are those of
   `catalog@2` (the base session's condition); until then every decision of
   the page is "not tested".
   - A class holds at the value it was found at: the matrix's `value` (its
     gold). A record of the kit with that value is drawn with the class; a
     record with another value is "not tested", and its panel and its line
     on the sheet name the value the run tried and its class. The matrix of
     the pilot is that of the house vector (`--vector house`, the house
     standard alone for the adverse event summary), so it classes the kit's
     records where they take the house's value (found after catalog-2c
     merged: 21 of the 67 records have a decision of the matrix, 14 of them
     at its value).
   - A run holds every other decision at the value of its vector, which may
     not be the kit's (the kit differs from the house vector in 7 of the 21
     decisions), and the reference build is Pilot 7's specifications, not a
     compiled one. So a class drawn carries a note that names the run's
     vector ("the P4 run of the vector house, every other decision at that
     vector's value"), and the legend says so (pass 3 of the review,
     P3-F1). A class `no_alternative` (the run had no other value to try)
     is drawn "not tested" with a note (P3-F2). A run on the kit's own values
     would need a vector of the kit's records in `specfirst gen separate`.

9. The gate `issue-31`: environment, pins, lint, unit tests, kit, P1,
   trace, grids (the three outputs of the acceptance and the other outputs
   of the kit), recompute (the panels' section 4), review (the page checks
   and the acceptance on Out14-1-1, Out14-3-1-1 and Out14-3-2-1, and the
   page of the second build of P1, byte for byte), decisions, planted
   defects (D7-display, D8-trace, D13-review), cache untouched, ASCII.

## Consequences

- A reviewer reads every number of the three tables from its calculation
  down to SDTM and to the decisions and documents behind it, offline, on
  one page; what is judged is drawn as judged.
- The pilot has decision records over a frozen catalog for the first time;
  they are a reading of the documents and of the build, held for form and
  quotes, and std-2 checks them by result.
- A new output of the kit is a new grid in `PILOT_GRIDS` and in the
  acceptance's list when it is reviewed; a new layout kind is an amendment
  of ADR-003.

## The owner's answers (2026-11-21, through the base session)

The owner answered in Chinese from the question dialog, each time the
recommended option; in the base session's translation:

- Q1 Records: write the pilot's records for the demographics and AE tables
  over catalog@2 / open-default@2, each stated with a SAP quote that a
  script checks or default with the house's source, held against the
  reference build; never in a reader's task, used by no score. Choosing it
  approves the kit format change: kit.yaml gets a `standard` field and the
  kit a second decisions file (ADR-001 item 3).
- Q2 Grids: #31 builds Out14-1-1 and Out14-3-2-1 first, with the two layout
  kinds they need, compared with the example's formatted values.
- Q3 Derivation: the panel shows the study spec's own Define-XML comment,
  labelled as such, next to the rule that runs, with its file and line.
- Q4 Approver: nobody yet; every judged edge is drawn "awaiting approval";
  approvals belong to the edit loop of PLAN.md section 7.6.
- The overlap with std-2: #31 writes the records of the two tables only,
  in safety.jsonl, held by the decisions check; std-2 takes the file over
  later, extends it to the whole pilot and adds its check by result;
  ADR-022 says so and does not claim std-2's check.

## The owner's answer (2026-11-22, through the base session)

Asked after the first review of issue #31 (F1), which found that the
records had not read the SAP's mock tables. The SAP says of them (13.1.3):
"Note that the templates that follow are for example only." The question
was how the records count them: A, a mock table states what it shows, its
masks included; B, its layout only (population, columns), not the
decimals of its masks; C, not at all. The owner chose A, the recommended
option, in Chinese from the question dialog; in the base session's
translation: what a mock table shows counts as what the SAP states, the
placeholder formats too; of the 49 records, 18 stated, 12 default, 17
conflict, 2 asked (decision 7). The conflicts stay for a person to
decide; this package resolves none.
