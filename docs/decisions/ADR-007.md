# ADR-007: The choices of gen-2a (gold and verification for the adverse event summary)

Status: decided in gen-2a, 2026-11-17, by the executor, where PLAN.md and
ADR-005 are silent. The decisions stand unless the owner objects. PLAN.md,
`standard/schemas/` and the catalog `catalog@1` are not changed. Five
points are for the owner, because they touch what other tracks own or go
beyond the words of the plan; they are listed at the end. Decision 6 was
approved by the owner on 2026-11-17, for the slice of ADR-005 only, with a
rework booked before the gate of the full gen-2: see "The owner's answer
on decision 6". The other four points are open.

## Context

gen-2a is the first part that joins three things that were built apart:
the compiler and the house standard of std-1, the result engines and the
grid of real-2 and real-3a, and the generated data of gen-1a. ADR-005 asks
for: decisions sampled within the house standard, compiled, built with both
engines on the visible data and on the twin, P1 to P4, a manifest and the
splits, for the one output `tfl-ae-summary`.

Three gaps showed when the parts met. Each needed a choice.

1. Nothing had ever run the result engines on an ARS file that the compiler
   wrote. It runs. But the house templates give a treatment grouping as
   data-driven, and the engines list the groups of a data-driven grouping
   from the records of each analysis (rule I9 of
   `standard/methods/README.md`). An arm in which no subject has a fatal
   event has no result for fatal events: not a zero. The decision
   `tfl.percent.format` is about exactly that zero.
2. The compiler writes a layout file from the templates of the house
   standard, which are "layout draft 0" (ADR-004, decision 6). The grid
   builder of real-3a reads layout spec v0 (ADR-003), another format. It has
   no total column, takes the order of the columns as a list and not as a
   rule, rounds half away from zero only, and has no rule for a count of
   zero. Four catalog entries of the unit decide exactly these:
   `tfl.columns.total`, `tfl.columns.arms`, `tfl.rounding.rule`,
   `tfl.percent.format`. ADR-004 gives the alignment to std-2.
3. A generated study has no reference. P2 says "the build equals the
   reference"; for a tier A kit the plan says "gold by construction".

## Decisions

1. **Listed groups.** Before the engines run, a data-driven grouping of the
   compiled ARS file is replaced by the list of the values its variable has
   in the analysis set, read from the grouping's own dataset
   (`specfirst.gen.gold.resolve_groupings`). Both engines then follow rule
   I8: every listed group gives a result, with or without records. The
   compiled file in `gold/spec/` keeps the rule (data-driven); the file
   with results in `gold/ars/` lists the groups of its data. A group has
   the ID `<grouping>_<n>`, counted in the sorted order of the values, so
   the ID of a result does not depend on the order of the columns; the
   `order` of a group is the place of its column.

   Not chosen: changing rule I9 in both engines. That is a change of the
   contract of real-2 for every kit; it may be the better end state, and it
   is the real track's to decide.

2. **The order of the columns needs the arms of the protocol.** The rule
   (`ascending_dose_placebo_first`, `protocol_order`) is a decision; the
   doses and the order in which the protocol lists the arms are not in any
   decision and not in SDTM. gen-2a takes them from the design of the kit
   (`gold.arms_of`: code, dose, and every text that can name the arm in a
   treatment column). A treatment label that names several arms (the
   treatment of the first exposure record) stands where the first of them
   stands. Layer L1 is a placeholder in M1 (section 4.1); this is the first
   place that needs something of it.

3. **A grid builder for layout draft 0** (`specfirst.gen.display`). It
   builds a grid of schema version 0 from the compiled layout and the ARD:
   the columns of the grouping in the order of its groups, a total column
   from its own grouping and analyses, the count under each column header
   as a cell of a row with the section `@header` (ADR-003, decision 4),
   cells of the kind `count_percent`, the rounding rule and the rule for a
   count of zero from the layout. A cell is "{n} {pct}" with the patterns
   "XX" and "(XXX.X)" of `display.pattern`: "12 ( 37.5)". It computes no
   number. It replaces nothing of the machine: the pilot kit is built by
   `specfirst.machine.grid` as before.

   Not chosen: extending layout spec v0 and its builder by a total column,
   a column rule, a rounding rule and a zero rule. That is the alignment
   ADR-004 gives to std-2 and the rest of real-3. When the house templates
   write a layout format that the machine's builder reads, this module
   goes.

4. **A rounding rule in the method registry.** The catalog offers
   `half_even` (`tfl.rounding.rule`); a display rule that a decision can
   choose is a registry entry (sections 5.2 and 7.3). New entry
   `display.round.half_even` with 17 vectors; `display.pattern` gets the
   option `rounding` and statement 8, added at the end. The default is
   unchanged: every text of the pilot kit is as before.

5. **The reference of a generated study is a hand calculation**
   (`specfirst.gen.reference`). It computes the adverse event summary from
   the SDTM rows and the decision values, by the words of the catalog
   entries: about 480 lines that share no code with the compiler, the
   templates, yamaa, the engines, the registry or the grid builder. P2 for
   a kit is: every cell, the columns and the rows of the grid equal the
   reference, on the visible data and on the twin, and for every mutated
   build of P4 as well. Where the words of an entry leave a choice the
   module marks its reading (`READING`); two are marked.

   Not chosen: P2 as "the two engines agree" or as "the grid is what the
   layout and the ARD give". Both hold a build against itself.

6. **What "applicable" means for the gate.** The gate text is "every
   applicable decision is separated on the twin". Two things are known of
   a decision in a kit before anything is built twice:

   - read: the output reads an element the decision sets. This is static:
     the binds of the compile, and the lineage of the ADaM columns behind
     the cells (`gold.reach`).
   - claimed: the log of probes says that the twin carries a probe that
     makes two alternatives differ in the output under the other decisions
     of this kit (the field `reaches`, evaluated by `probes.reaches`).

   A decision is applicable in a kit when every pair of its value with
   another alternative is claimed. A kit in which a claimed pair gives the
   same output is not written. A decision that is not read must change
   nothing under mutation; if it does, that is a defect. A decision that
   is read and not claimed is built all the same and recorded as it comes
   out. The one case of the slice: `tfl.rounding.rule` and
   `tfl.percent.format` are read by every table, but the calibrated counts
   that separate them hold for one analysis set and one grouping. In a kit
   on another analysis set the rounding rule is `unseparated`, and the
   scorer compares decision values there (section 9.3).

   Over the ten kits of the gate every pair of alternatives of every
   decision of the slice must be separated in at least one kit, except a
   decision whose reach is `never` (`date.studyday.origin`).

7. **Scenarios.** Kit n of a set is given some decisions
   (`decisions.SCENARIOS`: the analysis set of the table, the end of
   emergence, the grouping), so that over a set every decision is read and
   claimed somewhere. The other decisions are drawn: each is the house
   default, or another alternative with a chance of 0.45. A scenario may
   need a design (an efficacy-evaluable set, three arms); the design is
   then drawn again.

8. **What a kit of the slice holds beyond the units of the slice.** The
   other three outputs of the house are stated as not included. The
   decisions of `mod-demographics` are at the house default, because ADSL
   cannot be compiled without them. ADVS is not built: no output of the
   slice reads it.

9. **The decision records cite a standard-form document.** A decision that
   differs from the house default is `stated` and cites `gold/docs/sap.md`,
   which gen-2a renders from the stated decisions; a default cites the
   standards document of the house. The parser reads the document back to
   the stated decisions: the parser control of section 9.3 has something to
   run on. gen-3a owns the documents of a kit (protocol and SAP, natural
   form, tiers) and will replace this file.

10. **Splits.** Kit n of a set has the seed "<seed>/kit-<n>"; its place
    gives its split (four train, three dev, three test in ten). A sealed
    kit is generated one at a time with `--split sealed`. Held out for an
    exam (section 6.7): designs with an allocation ratio other than 1 to 1,
    and the combination "no total column" with "columns in protocol order".
    No train, dev or test kit has either. (Changed by ADR-020, catalog-2c,
    2026-11-22: under open-default@2 a table has no total column by default,
    and the combination held out is "a total over all arms" with "columns in
    protocol order", two stated alternatives as before.)

## Consequences

- `specfirst.machine.lineage` got two words (`part` as a literal key, DATE
  as a keyword). Without them every column behind an `imputation_flag` or
  a date literal of the compiled specs had a gap in the trace. The pilot
  specifications use neither; the tests of the pilot kit are part of the
  gate gen-2a.
- The data of a kit are still a function of the design and the seed alone
  (gen-1a). The decisions are sampled to fit the data, not the other way
  round. That is why a rounding rule cannot be separated in a kit on the
  efficacy-evaluable set.
- A decision vector is drawn again when its kit does not separate what the
  log claims. The manifest of a kit counts the vectors drawn before.

## For the owner

1. Decision 1 or a change of rule I9: which should stay. (real track)
2. Decision 3: who aligns the house layout templates with a layout format
   that carries the total column, the column rule, the rounding rule and
   the zero rule, and when. Until then `specfirst.gen.display` builds the
   grids of generated kits, and the machine's builder those of the pilot.
   (std-2, rest of real-3)
3. Decision 2: the alternative `protocol_order` of `tfl.columns.arms` has
   no slot for the order, so a build from decisions alone cannot order the
   columns. The catalog is frozen; this is noted for the std track.
4. Decision 4 adds to the registry of real-2. (real track)
5. Decision 6: the gate reads "applicable" as "claimed by the log of
   probes". With that reading, `tfl.rounding.rule` is unseparated in kits
   that are not on the safety set grouped by actual treatment. Answered:
   see below.

## The owner's answer on decision 6 (2026-11-17)

Pass 1 of the independent review did not sign gen-2a because of decision 6
(finding G1 of `docs/reports/gen-2a.review.md`). The gate passes kits in
which a decision that the output reads, and that other data could
separate, is not separated on the twin; the reading of the gate text that
allows it is the owner's to accept, not the executor's (PLAN.md section
2.3). The executor stopped and asked. The base session put the question to
the owner in the question dialog and sent the answer to the session of
gen-2a.

The question and the options were shown in Chinese. Rule R10 keeps this
file in ASCII, so they stand here in the executor's translation; the
owner's own words are in the transcripts of the two sessions.

Question: "The gate of gen-2a passes in full, but the review did not sign.
The reason: in 2 of the 10 kits (kits 4 and 9), one or two decisions each
(the rounding rule, the source of the treatment end date) cannot be told
apart on the twin data, although other data could have told them apart.
The plan asks that 'every applicable decision can be told apart on the
twin data'; gen-2a reads 'applicable' as 'what the generator claims it can
tell apart', and the review holds that this reading needs your approval.
Which do you choose?"

Chosen: "A. Approve the reading, merge today (recommended)", with the
description: "Accept ADR-007, decision 6, as the reading of the gate for
this slice. The decisions that cannot be told apart in those two kits are
scored by comparing the decision values directly (the kit marks each of
them). The rework is booked as a later part, to be done before the gate of
the full gen-2. The second half of read-1a, and gen-3a, can start at
once."

Not chosen: "B. Rework now."

What follows:

1. Decision 6 is the reading of the gate text "every applicable decision
   of the slice is separated on the twin" for the gate `gen-2a`, that is
   for the slice of ADR-005, and for nothing else. It is not a reading of
   the gate of the full gen-2 (PLAN.md section 13.5). PLAN.md is not
   changed.
2. The rework is owed before the gate of the full gen-2 is run. The
   decisions of a kit come first and its probes follow them (section 6.4:
   stage S1 before S3): the calibrated counts are set for the analysis set
   and the grouping of the kit's own table, and the planted subjects are
   inside its analysis set. `probes.REACHES` then keeps only the
   conditions under which another decision of the kit makes the
   alternatives equal on any data (kind (a) of finding G1), and the gate
   fails on a kit in which a decision that the output reads, and that data
   could separate, is not separated on the twin (kind (b)). The data of a
   kit will then depend on its decisions, which changes the generator of
   gen-1a: its gate runs again with the rework. Booked in
   `docs/state/gen.md` under "Next".
3. Until then a scorer compares decision values wherever
   `gold/identifiability.json` of a kit says `unseparated`, and reports
   those decisions apart (section 9.3). Under this reading that includes
   decisions which the output reads: in the ten kits of the gate,
   `tfl.rounding.rule` in kits 4 and 9 and `trt.end.source` in kit 9.
