# ADR-016: The first scores of the slice on dev kits, and the pilot as a reading task

Status: decided in the report `docs/reports/slice-ae-summary.md`
(ADR-005, decision 1, last paragraph), 2026-11-19, by the executor, where
PLAN.md, ADR-005 and ADR-010 are silent. PLAN.md, `standard/schemas/`,
`catalog@1` and the house standard `open-default@1` are not changed. The
owner chose the scope in the base session on 2026-11-19: four dev kits,
and at most USD 10 of model use computed from the tokens. The decisions
stand unless the owner objects.

## Context

ADR-005 puts a report after read-2a: the first scores of the slice
"adverse event summary first" on dev kits, and the report on the pilot of
its decision 5 (stage T on the reference ADaM of CDISCPILOT01, arms S and
R, arm R reading the real SAP). It is not a delivery; node N3 applies
before any of it is shown outside (PLAN.md section 13.1).

The harness of read-2a (ADR-010) runs an arm on a generated kit. A task of
a real study, a SAP that is a PDF without anchors, and a run on the pilot
were not built. The scorer of read-1a scores arm S on the pilot only. The
store of model text holds the statements of the ten kits of the gate seed;
the loop that adds others waits for the gen track (ADR-009, "The owner's
answers on items 4 and 5", point 2). ADR-014 lets arm S build the chain,
which makes "R against S" on the chain a comparison with something.

## Decisions

1. **Four dev kits from a dev seed.** The first four kits of a set that
   `SPLIT_PLAN` marks dev: kits 5, 6, 7 and 15 (tiers standard, full, the
   ask demo and standard, by `documents.TIER_PLAN`). The seed is a random
   text in a file outside every checkout (PLAN.md section 6.7), in a WSL
   folder that every run denies to the reader; the repository holds
   neither the seed nor its SHA-256. A run is given the seed of its kit
   (`<seed>/kit-<n>`, a file in the same folder) so that the canary scan
   has it. No kit of the gate seed is scored (gen-3a review, D7).

2. **One condition.** The reader runs with the condition `notes` (the
   core skill and the reading notes of the four units), as the dry run of
   read-2a did. The condition `catalog` and the comparison of the two are
   read-3's. Eight runs of arm R: four kits, two stages.

3. **The natural form as the store gives it.** A kit takes the accepted
   text of each statement from the store in the repository. A statement of
   a dev kit that the store does not have gives its phrasing template,
   marked `missing` (ADR-009, decision 5). The loop of the natural form is
   not run for the dev seed: it moves to `claude -p` first, with findings
   D13 to D15 of the gen-3a review settled (gen track). The scores are on
   the documents as they are, and the report counts the paragraphs by
   source.

4. **Nothing of a dev kit's runs is committed.** The records a reader
   writes are nearly the gold vector of the kit, and its transcript holds
   the kit's documents: both would let gold be rebuilt (PLAN.md section
   8). Run folders, task folders, transcripts and logs stay outside the
   repository (a Windows folder outside every checkout for tasks and runs,
   a WSL folder for kits and logs). The report gives numbers and the
   commands, never a value of a decision of a dev kit, and never a
   decision of a dev kit named beside its gold class, its status or
   anything else that, with the catalog and the house standard, gives its
   value: the decisions a reader got wrong are given by unit and class as
   counts. The first version of the report named one with its class
   (review, finding 1). The scores are given summed over the kits: with
   the public catalog and house templates, the cell counts of a single
   kit beside its number of arms can narrow its table decisions (review,
   pass 2). The scores of each kit stay in the records.

5. **The pilot as a reading task** (`specfirst.harness.pilot`). A folder
   of the shape of a kit, tier C, is written outside every checkout from
   the pinned files of `kits/cdiscpilot01` (their SHA-256 checked): the
   SAP as text, the house standard, the reference ADSL and ADAE as the
   ADaM of stage T, CDISC's ARS example and the key outside `inputs/`, an
   oracle with no answer (every table decision has a house default) and no
   gold decisions (the pilot's decision records are std-2's). The harness
   then runs an arm on it as on a kit, stage T only.

   - The SAP is the text poppler's `pdftotext` gives (7-bit ASCII, pinned
     with the system). Each paragraph, a run of lines that are not empty,
     gets the anchor `[<page>/p<n>]` in front: the page of the PDF and the
     number of the paragraph on it. Besides, lines lose their trailing
     blanks and every character that is not printable ASCII or a tab, the
     first line of a paragraph its leading blanks, and runs of empty lines
     and the form feeds become one empty line. A quote is checked against
     this text.
   - The protocol of the study is not public, so the task has the SAP
     alone. `TASK.md` of a real study differs from a kit's only in the
     lines on the documents and in the anchors of its two examples
     (`task.render_real`); the text of a kit's task is not changed, and a
     change of it that the swap does not find stops the build.
   - The canary sources are the files outside `inputs/`, as for a kit:
     the example (its analyses and the 3,735 results it embeds) and the
     key. The fetch cache lies in the WSL file system, which every run
     denies to a Windows reader.

6. **Arm R on the pilot is scored by its build.** The reader's seven table
   decisions, the house default for the rest, are built on the reference
   ADaM and held against the key, and every cell that differs is
   explained by another value of one table decision or listed, as for arm
   S (`score.pilot.run(machine, records, "R")`; ADR-008, decision 10).
   A candidate that leaves the output out (`tfl.output.include` =
   `not_included`) builds nothing to compare. Its trace sets that decision
   to the value that builds the output first, and explains each cell
   against that build: by that decision alone, or with one more table
   decision, or in a row that cannot be built (lesson 69). There is no
   reading score: the pilot has no gold decision records before std-2.
   The report lists the reader's records with their values and anchors,
   and quotes the SAP only as far as a short phrase proves a value (rule
   4). The run, its transcript and the folder stay outside the
   repository.

7. **The budget of the report.** A run of arm R starts only while the
   total computed cost of the runs of arm R so far, plus twice the dearest
   of them (at least USD 1), stays at or below USD 10. The budget of one
   run is that of ADR-010, decision 8.

## Consequences

- `specfirst run pilot --arm S|R --folder DIR [--score]` and `specfirst
  score pilot --run RUN`. The folder, the task and the run lie outside
  every checkout.
- The task of a generated kit is the same file for file; the kept dry run
  of read-2a still checks against the task built now.
- Dev kits are generated again, never kept in the repository. Their
  scores in the report can be made again from the seed file and the
  commit, with the model's answers being the only part that does not
  repeat.

## For the owner

Nothing is asked. The scope (four dev kits, USD 10) is the owner's choice
of 2026-11-19. Decision 3 means the first scores read documents of which
about one decision paragraph in eight is the phrasing template; the
report states it.

One offer, not taken without the owner: the reader of the pilot read the
real SAP as planning no adverse event summary (`tfl.output.include` =
`not_included`), which the SAP's list of displays supports, while the key
is the summary of CDISC's ARS example. A second pilot task that gives the
output as planned (that decision fixed at `included`, as stage T fixes the
module decisions at gold) would have the reader decide the other six table
decisions from the SAP: one more run of arm R, about USD 0.6.
