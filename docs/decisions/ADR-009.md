# ADR-009: The documents of a kit (gen-3a), and how a model reaches them

Status: decided in gen-3a, 2026-11-18, by the executor, where PLAN.md and
ADR-005 are silent. PLAN.md, `standard/schemas/` and the catalog
`catalog@1` are not changed. The decisions stand unless the owner objects;
the points for the owner are listed at the end.

## Context

gen-3a is the part of gen-3 for the slice of ADR-005: the protocol and the
SAP of each kit, in standard form and in natural form (PLAN.md section
6.6). Its gate is the gate of gen-3 for these documents: "the parser
recovers 100% of the decisions from every standard-form document. Every
natural document passes the slot check and the read-back check. A planted
bad rewrite (a changed number) is rejected. Each kit records which probes
are visible."

The natural form is written by a model (section 6.4: "generated once,
checked, frozen in the kit and identified by its hash"), and the read-back
check is a verifier agent with a fresh context. Rule R4 forbids a model in
a gate on a clean clone: the gate must give the same answer every time.
Rule R1: what an agent writes counts only after a deterministic check.

The hard point is how the rewrite and the read-back reach a model at all.

## Decisions

1. **The model is reached through subagents of the executor's Claude Code
   session.** Two routes were looked at.

   - `claude -p` of the Claude Code CLI, the route section 11.1 names for
     the reader of read-2. The CLI on this machine (npm, version 2.0.29)
     answers a print-mode call with "Invalid API key - Please run /login"
     (the call cost nothing: 0 tokens). It is not logged in. Logging in
     is the owner's act and handles a credential; this part does not do
     it.
   - Subagents of the session that runs gen-3a (the Agent tool of Claude
     Code), on the owner's subscription that the session already runs on.
     No credential is handled and no new service is used. Chosen.

   The model is Sonnet 5.5 for both roles (the subagent option `sonnet`):
   a cheaper model than the session's own, strong enough to write plain
   prose and to read it back. The cost of every call (tokens, tool uses,
   time, as the Agent tool reports them) is logged in
   `src/specfirst/gen/rewrites/calls.jsonl` and summed in the report.

   What a subagent cannot do: be called from a script. The loop of
   section 6.6 (rewrite, check, read back, regenerate up to three times,
   fall back) is therefore driven by the executor, one command at a time
   (`specfirst gen natural ...`, decision 6). Each command writes the
   request a subagent gets and reads the file it writes back; the scripts
   decide. When the owner logs the CLI in, a `claude -p` adapter can take
   the executor's place (read-2a builds one for the reader); the store
   of decision 5 does not change.

2. **Two documents, each in two forms.** The reader gets the natural form
   and cites it; gold holds the standard form.

   | File | Document ID | Form |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | `inputs/docs/protocol.md` | `protocol` | natural |
   | `inputs/docs/sap.md` | `sap` | natural |
   | `gold/docs/protocol.md` | `protocol-standard` | standard |
   | `gold/docs/sap.md` | `sap-standard` | standard |

   Every decision that the documents state is stated once, in the
   document of its entry (`documents.HOME`): the protocol states the
   analysis sets (`pop.randomized.rule`, `pop.safety.rule`,
   `pop.efficacy.rule`) and what makes an adverse event related
   (`ae.related.values`); the SAP states the rest. The natural protocol
   also states the design that a reader needs and that no decision holds:
   the arms in the order of the protocol with their codes, labels and
   doses, the visits with their planned days, the vital signs, the
   causality scale, the disposition subcategories. That text is rendered
   from `gold/design.yaml` by a script, without a model, and it is the
   same in every kit of a design. The standard-form documents hold
   decisions only.

3. **Anchors of the natural form.** Every paragraph of the body starts
   with an anchor `[<section>/p<n>]`, where `<section>` is the number of
   its heading (`6.1`) and n counts the paragraphs of the section. Design
   and filler paragraphs have anchors too, so an anchor does not show
   where a decision stands. A decision paragraph is one line. The span of
   an anchor is what the scorer already reads
   (`specfirst.score.reading.Documents.span`): the line after the anchor
   and the lines that follow up to the next empty line. The standard form
   keeps its anchors `[<section>/s<n>]` (ADR-004, decision 2).

   A stated decision cites both forms: first the standard-form statement
   (doc `<id>-standard`, its anchor, the statement as the quote), then the
   natural paragraph (doc `protocol` or `sap`, its anchor, the whole
   paragraph as the quote). The reading score accepts a citation of
   either (a citation names an anchor that gold cites, with a quote inside
   its span): the parser control cites the first, a reader the second.

4. **Tiers, by the number of a kit in its set** (`documents.TIER_PLAN`,
   ten places like the splits): full in kits 1, 3, 6, 9; standard in 2,
   5, 8, 10; the ask demo in 4 (train) and 7 (dev). A kit of its own
   draws full or standard from its seed, or takes `--documents`.

   - full: the documents state every decision of the kit, house defaults
     included. Every record is `stated`, class `document_rule`; this
     changes status and citations, never values (docs/state/gen.md).
   - standard: the documents state the decisions that differ from the
     house default; the others are `default` and cite the house standard
     (`house`), as in gen-2a.
   - ask: as standard, and every decision of the slice that has no house
     default (`ae.related.values` always; `pop.efficacy.rule` when the
     design has an efficacy-evaluable set) is left out of both documents.
     Its record has the status `asked`, the class `study_input`, the gold
     value and no citation (section 4.5; ADR-004, schema point 3 lets the
     answer be cited once a run has an ask ID). `gold/oracle.yaml` holds
     the answer the oracle gives (section 11.3). Every kit has the file;
     outside the ask demo its list is empty.

   The parser control (arm C) reads the standard-form documents and the
   house standard, and asks what neither states and has no house default;
   the oracle answers (`specfirst.score.scorer.control_records`). Without
   that, no control can score 100% on a kit of the ask demo. A candidate
   record with the status `asked` counts as asked when the run gives no
   list of questions; a run of a reader gives its questions file, and
   then only that counts.

5. **The store of model text.** `src/specfirst/gen/rewrites/` holds, for
   every statement that a kit of the gate states, one record
   (`paragraphs.jsonl`): the statement, every attempt (the text, the call
   that wrote it, the problems the slot check found, the alternative the
   verifier read back and the call that read it), and which attempt is
   accepted. `filler.jsonl` holds the neutral filler paragraphs and their
   read-back. The store is append-only: an accepted attempt is never
   changed. A kit takes the accepted text of each of its statements,
   records in `gold/docs/natural.json` where each paragraph of its
   natural documents came from, with the SHA-256 of its text, and the
   manifest names the SHA-256 of every file as before. A statement
   without an accepted text gives its phrasing template, marked
   `missing`: such a kit fails the gate. The gate never calls a model: it
   runs the slot check again and holds the recorded read-back against
   gold, for exactly the text of each paragraph (by hash).

   The store of the gate's kits is in the repository: their seed is a
   development seed and is in the repository too. For dev, test and
   sealed kits the store is a folder outside every checkout
   (`SPECFIRST_REWRITES`): the statements of a secret vector stay out.

6. **The loop of section 6.6, as commands.**

   - `specfirst gen natural requests KITS --out FILE`: the statements of
     the kits that have no accepted text and fewer than three failed
     attempts, with the meaning of their alternative and the rules of
     the rewrite.
   - A subagent (the rewriter) writes one paragraph per statement into a
     proposals file.
   - `specfirst gen natural propose FILE --call ID`: the slot check runs
     on each proposal; the attempt is stored with its problems.
   - `specfirst gen natural readback-requests --out FILE`: every attempt
     that passed the slot check and has no read-back, without the
     statement and without the answer: the paragraph, the question of
     its entry and the definitions of its alternatives, under an opaque
     item ID.
   - A new subagent (the verifier, a fresh context that has never seen
     the standard form) picks one alternative per item, or "none".
   - `specfirst gen natural readback FILE --call ID`: an attempt whose
     pick is the alternative of its statement is accepted. A statement
     with three failed attempts gets its phrasing template (the `plain`
     family of `standard/catalog/phrasings/`, rendered with the slots of
     its value), which is read back like any attempt.

   The rewriter is told: one paragraph of one to three sentences in the
   voice of a protocol or SAP; keep every number, unit, visit name,
   variable name, quoted value and the name of the output as written, in
   the order of the statement; add no rule, no example and no value; ASCII
   only. Filler is written the same way, once, as a small library of
   neutral paragraphs (introduction, data handling, software, quality
   control, changes to the plan); the verifier must find that a filler
   paragraph states none of the decisions of the slice.

7. **The slot check** (`specfirst.gen.natural.slot_problems`). The tokens
   of a statement are, in its order: every scalar of every slot value (the
   items of a list, the codes and labels of a map), and from the text of
   the template the variable names (`DM.ARMCD`), the numbers, the unit
   word after a number (`days`, `years`), and for an entry of a table
   family the name of the output. Each must stand in the paragraph as
   whole words, ignoring case, quotes and blanks, and in the order of the
   statement: a list in another order, or two labels exchanged in a map,
   is a changed meaning. The paragraph must be one line of ASCII with no
   anchor and no heading mark. The read-back check is the other half:
   the alternative, which no token shows.

8. **Each kit records which probes are visible.** `kit.yaml` lists the
   probes of the log by dataset (`probes: visible, twin`). The gate finds
   every visible probe again in the visible SDTM
   (`specfirst.gen.present.coverage`, gen-1a).

9. **A one-page summary per kit**, `gold/summary.md`, for people (the
   sample of section 6.6 that the owner reads at N3): the study and its
   arms, the tier, every decision with its status and where it is stated
   (both anchors), the identifiability of each, the visible probes. At
   most 60 lines of at most 100 characters. It is gold: it names the
   decisions.

10. **The generator is `gen-3a.1`.** Documents change the bytes of every
    kit; the decision vectors of a seed do not change (the documents are
    written from a vector, after it is drawn). The gates gen-2a and
    read-1a run on the new kits of their seed.

## The gate gen-3a

`specfirst gate gen-3a` writes the ten kits of the seed of the gate of
gen-2a (their statements are the statements of the store) and checks:

1. parser: every standard-form document parses, and the decisions it
   states are exactly the decisions the records mark `stated` in it, with
   the anchors they cite; 100% of the decisions of the kit are recovered
   from the documents together with the house standard and the oracle;
2. slots: every decision paragraph of every natural document passes the
   slot check;
3. read-back: every decision paragraph is the accepted text of the store
   for its statement, and the verifier's recorded pick for that text is
   the gold alternative; every filler paragraph was read back as stating
   nothing;
4. a planted bad rewrite (a number changed) is rejected by the slot check
   and by the read-back check (no read-back for that text);
5. tiers: full, standard and ask demo as decision 4; two kits of the ask
   demo; the oracle answers exactly the asked decisions;
6. citations: every quote of every gold citation stands inside the span
   of its anchor;
7. visible probes: listed in `kit.yaml`, equal to the log, each found in
   the visible data;
8. summary: one page, every decision named.

Each check is first shown to fail on a planted defect (rule R5).

## Consequences

- Model text is in the repository: the accepted paragraphs of the
  statements of the gate's kits and the filler, with their read-back.
  It is our own text (MIT) and ASCII.
- One rewrite per statement: kits that state the same decision the same
  way share the paragraph. The natural form varies with the order of
  sections and paragraphs, the filler and the design, not with the
  wording of a statement. More rewrites per statement, chosen by the
  seed of a kit, fit the store as it is.
- The natural form measures robustness to wording only (section 6.6, the
  known limit), and here to one wording per statement.
- The isolation of the verifier is by its request file (no statement, no
  gold) and by its instructions; a subagent could read files of the
  machine. Its transcript is not kept.
