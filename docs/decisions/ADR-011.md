# ADR-011: Organ class and term stay data-driven; only groupings of the subject population are listed

Status: decided in the fix of issue #27 (track real), 2026-11-18, by the
executor. PLAN.md, `standard/schemas/`, the catalog `catalog@1`, the house
standard `open-default@1`, the method registry and its rules I1 to I15 are
not changed. The decision narrows decision 1 of ADR-007 and stands unless
the owner objects. Item 1 of ADR-007's "For the owner" (decision 1 or a
change of rule I9) stays open; see "For the owner".

## Context

Issue #27: the compiled ARS file of `tfl-ae-soc-pt` groups the analysis by
preferred term (`An_AESOCPT_Pt`) by treatment, organ class (`Grp_AESOC`,
ADAE.AESOC) and term (`Grp_AEDECOD`, ADAE.AEDECOD). Each term lies in one
organ class, but a build gave a count for every arm, every organ class and
every term: on invented data with 20 organ classes and 200 terms (200
pairs occur) 12,000 counts where 600 are the table, and a build 50 times
as long as at 10 x 50.

The compiled file is right. The house template (`ars/ae-soc-pt.yaml`)
writes both term groupings as data-driven, "as in the CDISC ARS example",
and rule I9 says what the engines do with them: "Several data-driven
groupings give the combinations of values that occur together on a record,
not every combination." The cross product came from the step between the
compiler and the engines. Decision 1 of ADR-007
(`specfirst.gen.gold.resolve_groupings`) replaced every data-driven
grouping of the compiled file by the list of the values its variable has
in the analysis set, so that an arm without a record still gets a count of
zero. It listed the two term groupings too, and with listed groups rule
I10 takes every combination of one group per grouping: arms x organ
classes x terms.

## What the public ARS model offers (rule R2)

Read in the pinned repository `ars` (commit 2c9b4b6), `model/ars_ldm.yaml`,
and in its two examples `workfiles/examples/ARS v1/*.json`. The commands
and their output are in docs/reports/issue-27.md.

- A grouping (`GroupingFactor`: `id`, `groupingDataset`,
  `groupingVariable`, `dataDriven`, `groups`) has one variable. Its
  description: "A factor used to subdivide either the subject population or
  data records in an analysis dataset for analysis."
- An analysis names its groupings as `OrderedGroupingFactor` (`order`,
  `groupingId`, `resultsByGroup`). There is no parent grouping, no nesting
  and no dependence between groupings. The words nest, hierarchy, parent
  and depend occur in the model only for `NestedList` (the list of
  contents) and `LevelOrder` (the place of a where clause or a list item in
  a tree).
- A defined group is a where clause, and a `CompoundGroupExpression` may
  combine conditions, also by reference to another group
  (`ReferencedGroup`). So a group "organ class x AND term y" can be
  written, but only as a listed group of one grouping.
- The example "Common Safety Displays" models the table exactly as our
  house does: treatment `AnlsGrouping_01_Trt` with three defined groups,
  organ class `AnlsGrouping_06_Soc` and term `AnlsGrouping_07_Pt`
  data-driven. Its analysis `An07_10_SocPt_Summ_ByTrt` embeds 690 counts:
  3 arms x the 230 pairs that occur. The engines of real-2 reproduce them
  with rules I9 and I10.
- In both examples every grouping on ADSL (treatment, sex, age group, race,
  ethnicity: 5 of the first example's 9 groupings and all 6 of the second)
  has defined groups, and so do parameter and visit of ADVS. The only
  data-driven groupings are AESOC and AEDECOD of ADAE.

## Options

1. Nest the term in the organ class in the compiled ARS file. The model
   has no way to say it; it would be an extension of our own in the files
   we write, and a change of the house template (`standard/`).
2. One grouping on the pair. A data-driven grouping has one variable, so
   it needs a derived ADaM variable (a change of the house standard), or a
   grouping with listed groups "AESOC EQ x AND AEDECOD EQ y" written before
   the engines run. The second fits the model, but the analysis that runs
   would have other groupings than the compiled one, the results other
   IDs, and the layout's `nesting: [Grp_AESOC, Grp_AEDECOD]` would point
   at groupings the event no longer uses.
3. Keep both term groupings data-driven, as compiled, and list only the
   groupings that need listed groups. Rule I9 gives the pairs that occur,
   rule I10 crosses them with every listed arm: arms x pairs, with a zero
   for an arm that has no record of a pair. This is the model of CDISC's
   example, which the engines already reproduce. Nothing under
   `standard/`, no rule and no engine changes.

## Decision

Option 3.

1. `specfirst.gen.gold.resolve_groupings` lists the groups of a
   data-driven grouping only when its dataset is ADSL, the subject-level
   analysis dataset of ADaM (one record per subject). Such a grouping
   subdivides the subject population: every one of its groups needs a
   result, an arm without a fatal event a count of zero (ADR-007). A
   data-driven grouping on another dataset subdivides the records of that
   dataset (ADAE.AESOC, ADAE.AEDECOD) and stays data-driven in the event
   the engines run. How a group is listed (the values in the analysis set,
   the ID `<grouping>_<n>`, the order the layout asks for) is unchanged.
2. Why "on ADSL" and not "the column grouping of a layout". On the four
   house templates both rules list the treatment groupings and leave the
   term groupings. They differ on the categorical rows of demographics
   (`Grp_SEX` and the others, data-driven on ADSL): "on ADSL" keeps them
   listed, so the IDs of their results stay as they are, and it is what
   CDISC's examples do with every grouping on ADSL. It also keeps the
   choice out of the layout: a decision that changes a layout does not
   change the IDs of results.
3. The engines and rules I8 to I10 are not changed. The compiled file in a
   kit's `gold/spec/` keeps every grouping as the decisions wrote it; the
   file with results in `gold/ars/` lists the groups of the groupings on
   ADSL.

## Consequences

- `tfl-ae-soc-pt`: the analysis by term gives arms x the pairs that occur,
  the analysis by organ class arms x the organ classes that occur. On the
  two cases of the issue the counts by term are 150 and 600 (were 1,500
  and 12,000), and a build takes 0.1 s and 0.2 s (was about 2 s and 65 s;
  docs/reports/issue-27.md). Both engines give the same 1,772 results on
  the larger case.
- An organ class or term that occurs on a record of the analysis set but
  on no record of the analysis (here: no treatment-emergent record) had a
  row of zeros and now has none. Rule I9 reads the records of the analysis,
  as CDISC's example and the tables of a CSR do.
- A result of a data-driven group carries the value in its ID
  (`.../Grp_TRT01A_1/=SOC%2001/=PT%2001-03`), not a group ID. No kit, test
  or grid had results of the term groupings: `tfl-ae-soc-pt` is outside
  the slice of ADR-005, and its layout (draft 0, `nesting`) is not built
  by `specfirst.gen.display`.
- Generated kits of the slice: the slice compiles the adverse event
  summary only, whose one data-driven grouping is on ADSL. Their files do
  not change (kits 1 to 4 of the seed `gen-2a`, written before and after:
  docs/reports/issue-27.md). The task folder of a reader (read-2a) holds
  the catalog, the registry, the schemas, the skills and such a kit: none
  of them changes.
- CDISCPILOT01 is built from CDISC's example and never goes through
  `resolve_groupings`: unchanged (gates real-2 and real-3a).
- `Build.groups` of the scorer (`specfirst.score.candidate`) has no group
  of the term groupings. A cell of the table by term is found by its
  values; the rows per group of a nested data-driven grouping are the
  layout work of real-4.
- Limit: a column grouping on a dataset other than ADSL (adverse events by
  severity as columns, if a template made it data-driven) is not listed.
  `specfirst.gen.display` then stops with a message that says so. A house
  template gives such a grouping defined groups, or real-4 widens the rule.

## For the owner

Nothing here waits for the owner. Item 1 of ADR-007 ("decision 1 or a
change of rule I9: which should stay", real track) stays open; this ADR
answers neither way. Whichever stays, a grouping of data records has to
give the combinations that occur: if rule I9 is changed instead of listing
groups, the change has to keep that part of it.
